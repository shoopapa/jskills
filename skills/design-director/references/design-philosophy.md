# Expertise with restraint

## Content sets the form
A coordinator needs fast, reliable instructions; a board needs a defensible decision; a customer needs to recognize a benefit. Design the reading and action sequence around that job. A beautiful composition that makes the task harder has failed.

## Distinctive through decisions
Specificity comes from the content's own structure: an actual production poster, a meaningful comparison, a precise annotation, a distinctive headline break. Do not manufacture distinctiveness with arbitrary geometry or an unrelated fashionable font.

## One dominant idea
Give the most important message or action the clearest visual weight. Secondary content should support it through proximity, scale, and sequence. A page where every element demands attention has no hierarchy.

## Restraint is active
Remove elements that do not carry information, enable action, or establish necessary structure. Keep expressive choices that improve comprehension or create an appropriate emotional tone. Minimalism does not mean low contrast, tiny type, or unexplained controls.

## Systems leave room for judgment
Use a repeatable spacing rhythm, a small type scale, consistent action styles, and deliberate color roles. Make optical adjustments where mathematical alignment looks wrong. Not every section needs the same container or identical layout.

## Boldness has a budget
Choose a focal move: a strong type contrast, unusually clear composition, or a vivid data emphasis. Let the rest be quiet. More techniques rarely mean more craft.

## Finish includes behavior
Error messages, focus states, stale results, long labels, print pagination, and mobile wrapping belong to the design. A screenshot of the happy path is only partial evidence.
